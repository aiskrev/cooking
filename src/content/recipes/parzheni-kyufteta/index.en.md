steps:
  - title: Soak the bread
    text: Soak the bread in water for 5 minutes and squeeze it out very well.
    time: 5
  - title: Knead the mince
    text: Combine the mince with the grated onion, the bread, the egg, the garlic, the parsley and the spices. Knead for 2–3 minutes, until the mixture binds.
    why: Brief kneading keeps the kyufteta soft; kneading for too long makes them rubbery.
  - title: Rest the mixture
    text: Cover the bowl and leave it in the refrigerator for at least 30 minutes.
    why: The mixture firms up and the kyufteta are easy to shape without falling apart.
    time: 30
  - title: Shape the kyufteta
    text: With dampened hands shape balls of about 70 g each and flatten them slightly. Roll them in the flour and shake off the excess.
  - title: Fry
    text: Heat the oil over medium heat and fry the kyufteta for 5–6 minutes on each side to a dark golden crust. Lift them onto kitchen paper.
    cue: There is no pink inside, and the juice that comes out when pierced is clear.
    avoid: Do not overcrowd the pan – the oil cools and the kyufteta are scalded instead of fried.
    time: 12
  - title: Serve
    text: Serve hot, with fried potatoes and shopska salad.
tips:
  - If the mixture sticks to your hands, dampen them with cold water.
  - The kyufteta can be shaped the day before and fried fresh.
mistakes:
  - Bread that is too wet – the kyufteta fall apart in the pan.
  - Too high a heat – dark outside, raw inside.
substitutions:
  - Only pork or only beef mince.
  - Instead of frying – in a preheated oven at 200 °C for about 25 minutes.
plating: The kyufteta piled in the middle of the plate, the fried potatoes and the salad on the side.
serving: Hot, with cold shopska salad and beer or ayran.
storage: Up to 2 days in the refrigerator; reheat in the oven, not in the microwave.
