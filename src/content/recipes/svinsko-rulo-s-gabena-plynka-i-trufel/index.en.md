mise:
  - Cut the meat lengthways into a sheet about 1.5 cm thick and pound it evenly.
  - Chop the mushrooms finely.
  - Switch the oven on to 180 °C.
technique: The mushroom stuffing is cooked dry and dense, the meat is rolled tightly around it and tied. The roulade is seared, finished in the oven and rested before cutting.
temperature: Oven at 180 °C (160 °C fan), 35–40 minutes. Pork is done at 63 °C in the middle, after which it rests for 3 minutes.
balance: The mushroom stuffing is earthy and deep, the truffle aromatic, the celeriac and the potatoes soft and sweet, and the wine sauce brings acidity.

steps:
  - title: Cook the stuffing
    text: Melt the butter, sweat the shallots and the garlic for 2 minutes and add the mushrooms and the thyme. Cook for 15 minutes until all the liquid has evaporated. Add the truffle paste and the salt and let it cool.
    cue: The pan is dry and the stuffing is dark and dense.
    avoid: A wet stuffing makes the roulade soggy and uneven.
    time: 15
  - title: Roll the roulade
    text: Lay out the meat, arrange the bacon, spread with the stuffing and roll up tightly. Tie with string every 3 cm.
    why: Tight rolling holds the shape and keeps the stuffing from leaking out.
  - title: Sear and roast
    text: Heat the oil and sear the roulade on all sides for 6–8 minutes. Transfer to the oven and roast for 35–40 minutes.
    cue: The thermometer shows 63 °C in the middle.
    time: 40
  - title: Cook the purée
    text: Boil the diced celeriac and potatoes for 20–25 minutes, rice them and stir in the butter and the hot milk until smooth.
    time: 25
  - title: Make the sauce
    text: In the pan used for the roulade pour in the wine and reduce it by half. Add the stock and boil to a syrup. Off the heat, whisk in the cold butter.
    time: 10
  - title: Rest and slice
    text: Let the roulade rest for 10 minutes, remove the string and cut it into thick slices with a sharp knife.
    time: 10
  - title: Serve
    text: Spread the purée, place a slice of roulade on it, spoon over the sauce and garnish with fresh leaves and thin slices of truffle or a drop of truffle oil.
tips:
  - If there is no truffle, use dried porcini, soaked and chopped.
  - The roulade can be rolled the day before and roasted just before serving.
mistakes:
  - Cutting at once – the stuffing falls apart.
  - Overcooking – lean pork dries out.
substitutions:
  - Chicken breast or turkey instead of pork – roast to 74 °C.
  - Potato mash instead of celeriac purée.
plating: Two slices showing the dense stuffing, on the purée, dark sauce around, truffle and flowers for garnish.
serving: With red wine and a green salad.
storage: The roasted roulade – up to 2 days in the refrigerator. Reheat in a sauce.
