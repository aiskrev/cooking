mise:
  - Boil the gooseberry pickle (vinegar, water, sugar, salt), cool it and pour it over the halved gooseberries. Leave for at least an hour.
  - Mix the salt, sugar and lemon zest for the cure.
  - Chill the plates – the dish is served cold.
technique: Filleting and pin-boning, a short dry cure, blowtorching, quick-pickling fruit, cucumber ribbons, a cold horseradish cream.
temperature: The cure takes place in the refrigerator (2–4 °C) for 30–40 minutes. Hold the torch 5–8 cm from the skin until it blisters and darkens in places – about 30–40 seconds per fillet. The core stays raw and cool.
balance: The oily mackerel is balanced by the sour gooseberries and vinegar, the cool cucumber and cream soften it, and the horseradish adds heat. The salt from the cure is enough – do not salt the fish again.

steps:
  - title: Lay out the ingredients
    text: Arrange the mackerel on ice, the gooseberries, cucumber, horseradish, cream, lemon and dill.
    photo: 01-skumriya-stapka-1.webp
  - title: Fillet the fish
    text: Remove the fillets with a thin flexible knife, starting behind the head and following the backbone.
    photo: 02-skumriya-stapka-2.webp
  - title: Remove the bones
    text: Cut out the thin strip of pin bones down the middle of the fillet in a V, or pull them with tweezers. Trim off the belly bones.
    why: The mackerel is eaten almost raw – every bone is noticeable.
    photo: 03-skumriya-stapka-3.webp
  - title: Cure
    text: Lay the fillets skin-side down in a tray, sprinkle evenly with the salt, sugar and lemon-zest mix and leave in the refrigerator for 30–40 minutes.
    cue: The flesh is firm and slightly paler.
    time: 35
    photo: 04-skumriya-stapka-4.webp
  - title: Rinse and dry
    text: Rinse off the cure quickly under cold water, pat very dry and sprinkle with the dill.
    avoid: Do not leave it longer – the fish becomes too salty.
    photo: 05-skumriya-stapka-5.webp
  - title: Prepare the cucumber
    text: Cut the cucumber into long ribbons with a peeler and some of it into dice. Season with the salt and vinegar just before serving.
    photo: 06-skumriya-stapka-6.webp
  - title: Make the cream
    text: Whisk the cream with the horseradish, dill and lemon juice and season. Chill.
    cue: The cream is thick, smooth and sharp.
    photo: 07-skumriya-stapka-7.webp
  - title: Torch
    text: Place the fillets skin-side up on a rack or metal tray and torch them until the skin blisters and darkens in places.
    cue: The skin is glossy and spotted dark, while the flesh beneath is still raw.
    time: 2
    photo: 08-skumriya-stapka-8.webp
  - title: Plate
    text: Slice the fillets on the diagonal into 3–4 pieces. Put a spoonful of cream on the plate, the mackerel on top, the cucumber ribbons, gooseberries and sprigs of dill. Grate horseradish over and add a drop of olive oil.
    photo: 09-skumriya-stapka-9.webp
tips:
  - Buy the mackerel on the day; the eyes should be clear and the gills bright red.
  - If you have no blowtorch, sear only the skin for 30 seconds in a very hot pan.
mistakes:
  - Curing too long – the fish turns salty and dry.
  - Torch held too close – the skin burns before the fat melts.
substitutions:
  - Instead of gooseberries – green apple or sour red cranberries.
  - Instead of crème fraîche – thick yogurt.
  - Instead of mackerel – salmon or bonito.
plating: "A light ceramic plate: a pool of cream, slices of mackerel laid on the diagonal, cucumber rolls, gooseberry halves, dill and grated horseradish."
serving: Cold, as a starter, with a glass of dry white wine or ice-cold vodka.
storage: Cured but untorched mackerel keeps for 1 day in the refrigerator; torch it just before serving.
