mise:
  - Ask the fishmonger to fillet and butterfly the eel – its blood is toxic when raw and must be handled carefully, with gloves.
  - Rinse the rice until the water runs clear and leave it to drain for 30 minutes.
  - Soak the bamboo skewers in water.
technique: A tare reduction; steaming the eel before grilling (Kanto style); grilling over charcoal with repeated glazing; Japanese rice.
temperature: The tare simmers gently for 15–20 minutes to a syrupy consistency. The eel is steamed for 8–10 minutes and grilled over embers or under a hot grill for 3–4 minutes per side, glazed 3–4 times.
balance: The sweet-salty tare and the rich eel need acidity and heat – umeboshi and sansho pepper provide them. The rice is a neutral background.

    text: Cook the rice with the water over a low heat, covered, for 12 minutes, leave off the heat for 10 minutes and fluff.
    time: 22
    photo: 03-unagi-stapka-3.webp
  - title: Cut and skewer
    text: Cut the eel into rectangles and thread them crosswise onto 2–3 skewers so they do not curl. Steam for 8–10 minutes.
    why: Steaming renders some of the fat and makes the flesh tender.
    time: 10
    photo: 04-unagi-stapka-4.webp
  - title: Grill and glaze
    text: Grill over the embers, skin side first, for 3–4 minutes, then flesh side. Brush with tare and turn; repeat 3–4 times.
    cue: The surface is dark, glossy and caramelised, blistered in places.
    avoid: Do not hold it over a flame – the sugar in the tare burns and turns bitter.
    photo: 05-unagi-stapka-5.webp
  - title: Serve
    text: Put the rice into bowls, sprinkle with shiso and add one umeboshi each. Remove the skewers, set the eel beside the rice, drizzle with a little tare and sprinkle with sansho and spring onion.
    photo: 06-unagi-stapka-6.webp
tips:
  - Japanese shops sell ready-grilled eel (kabayaki) – it only needs warming and glazing.
  - Leftover tare keeps in the refrigerator for months.
  - If you have no charcoal, use the oven grill on maximum.
mistakes:
  - Eel without skewers – it curls and cooks unevenly.
  - Tare too thin – it does not cling or caramelise.
  - Grilling over an open flame – a bitter, burnt taste.
substitutions:
  - Instead of eel – mackerel or salmon, glazed the same way.
  - Instead of umeboshi – finely chopped pickled gherkins.
plating: "A dark ceramic plate or box: rice, the glossy pieces of eel, umeboshi, sansho and spring onion."
serving: "Hot. Suitable non-alcoholic pairings: cold Japanese barley tea (mugicha) or sencha green tea."
storage: The eel is best at once. Cooked and cooled, it keeps in the refrigerator for up to 1 day and is reheated under the grill.
