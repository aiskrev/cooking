mise:
  - Separate the leaves of the sauerkraut; if it is very sour, soak them for 15 minutes in cold water.
  - Rinse the rice.
technique: The leaves are filled, rolled tightly and braised under a weight, in a little liquid.
temperature: Low heat, covered – the liquid barely moves. A chicken filling is safe at 74 °C at least.
balance: A sour freshness from the cabbage, a tender filling and the cool creamy softness of the yogurt.

steps:
  - title: Sweat the onion
    text: Sweat the onion in the oil for 3 minutes, take it off the heat and add the paprika.
    avoid: Do not leave the paprika over high heat – it burns and turns bitter.
    time: 3
  - title: Make the filling
    text: Mix the mince, the rice, half of the onion, the spices and the salt.
  - title: Roll the sarmi
    text: Cut out the thick rib of the leaves, put a heaped tablespoon of filling on each and roll tightly, folding the ends inwards.
  - title: Arrange
    text: Pack the sarmi tightly into the clay pot. Add the remaining onion, the tomato purée and the water.
  - title: Braise
    text: Cover the sarmi with a plate so that they do not float up, and braise them over low heat for 60–70 minutes.
    cue: The rice is soft and the leaves are translucent and tender.
    time: 65
  - title: Serve
    text: Serve hot, with cold yogurt and dill.
tips:
  - Tight rolling and the plate on top keep the sarmi from loosening.
  - The yogurt goes on at serving, not during cooking.
mistakes:
  - Too much rice – the filling swells and tears the leaves.
  - High heat – the sauce boils away and the sarmi stick.
substitutions:
  - Pork or mixed mince instead of chicken.
  - Fresh cabbage, blanched beforehand.
plating: In a clay bowl, 3–4 sarmi per portion, with a spoonful of yogurt and dill.
serving: Hot, with cold yogurt and bread.
storage: Up to 3 days in the refrigerator; the next day they are even tastier.
allergens:
