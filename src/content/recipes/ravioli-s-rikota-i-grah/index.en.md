mise:
  - Drain the ricotta in a sieve for 30 minutes – a wet filling tears the dough.
  - Put a large pot of water on to boil and salt it well.
technique: The dough is kneaded for a long time, rested and rolled out into a thin sheet. When sealing, the air is pressed out with the fingers around the filling – otherwise the ravioli burst in the water.
temperature: The water boils gently, not violently. Fresh ravioli cook for 3–4 minutes – they are done a minute after they float.
balance: The ricotta is soft and milky, the lemon zest lifts it, the brown butter adds a nutty note and the peas sweetness.

steps:
  - title: Make the dough
    text: Make a well in the flour, break the eggs with the salt into it and stir with a fork, gradually drawing in the flour. Knead for 8–10 minutes, wrap the dough in cling film and leave it to rest for 30 minutes.
    cue: The dough is smooth, firm and springs back when pressed with a finger.
    time: 30
  - title: Roll out
    text: Divide the dough into four and pass each part through the pasta machine, from the widest setting to the second to last, dusting lightly with flour. Keep the finished sheets covered.
    cue: The sheet is so thin that you can see your hand through it.
  - title: Fill and seal
    text: Stir the ricotta with the Parmesan, the lemon zest, the nutmeg, salt and black pepper. Place a teaspoon of filling every 4 cm on one sheet, moisten the dough around it, cover with the second sheet and press with your fingers, pushing out the air. Cut out the ravioli and arrange them on a tray dusted with semolina.
    avoid: Air left inside expands in the water and the ravioli burst.
  - title: Boil
    text: Drop the ravioli into gently boiling salted water, in two batches, and cook them for 3–4 minutes. The peas go into the water for the last minute.
    cue: The ravioli have floated up, and their edge is soft but still firm.
    time: 4
  - title: Make the brown butter
    text: Melt the butter in a wide frying pan and let it foam until it smells of hazelnuts and the milk solids on the bottom turn brown. Take it off the heat and add the lemon juice and a spoonful or two of the pasta water.
    cue: The butter is amber and smells of toasted nuts.
  - title: Serve
    text: Transfer the ravioli and the peas with a slotted spoon straight into the pan, shake to coat them in the butter and serve with grated Parmesan.
tips:
  - Without a machine the dough can be rolled with a long rolling pin – it takes longer, but it works.
  - Freeze the extra ravioli in a single layer on a tray and then transfer them to a bag; they are cooked straight from frozen, a minute longer.
mistakes:
  - Air in the ravioli – they burst.
  - Boiling hard – it tears the thin dough.
substitutions:
  - Izvara (fresh curd cheese), well drained and rubbed through a sieve, instead of ricotta.
  - A few sage or mint leaves in the brown butter.
plating: Six or seven ravioli in a deep plate, the peas scattered, the butter on top and a cloud of Parmesan.
serving: Immediately, with a glass of dry white wine.
storage: Uncooked ravioli – up to a day in the refrigerator, dusted with semolina, or up to a month in the freezer.
