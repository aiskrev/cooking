mise:
  - Cut the onion into thin half-moons.
  - Grease a tin about 30 cm in diameter with oil.
technique: The onion is sweated slowly without burning and combined with the eggs once it has cooled. The dough rests and is rolled out into four thin sheets, which are layered with the filling.
temperature: Oven at 190 °C (170 °C fan), 35–40 minutes, until the crust is golden.
balance: Slowly sweated onion is sweet; the paprika and the black pepper make it piquant, and the yogurt on the side freshens it.

steps:
  - title: Prepare the ingredients
    text: Lay out the flour, the onion, the eggs, the oil and the spices.
  - title: Knead and roll out the dough
    text: Knead a soft dough from the flour, the water, the oil, the vinegar and the salt and knead it for 8 minutes. Divide it into 4 balls, brush them with oil and leave them to rest for 30 minutes. Roll each out into a thin sheet, slightly larger than the tin.
    cue: The rested dough rolls out easily and does not spring back.
    time: 30
  - title: Sweat the onion
    text: Sweat the onion with the salt in 60 ml of the oil over low heat for 15–20 minutes, stirring. Take it off the heat, add the paprika and the black pepper and let it cool. Stir the eggs into the lukewarm filling.
    cue: The onion is soft, golden and sweet.
    avoid: Burnt onion turns bitter – if it starts to darken, add a spoonful of water.
    time: 20
  - title: Assemble the banitsa
    text: Lay the first sheet in the tin so that its edges hang over. Spread a third of the filling, cover with a second sheet and continue. Finish with the fourth sheet, tuck the edges inwards and brush with the remaining oil.
  - title: Bake
    text: Prick the banitsa with a fork in several places and bake it for 35–40 minutes at 190 °C.
    cue: The banitsa is golden on top and pulls away from the sides of the tin.
    time: 40
  - title: Serve
    text: Cover the banitsa with a cloth for 10 minutes, cut it and serve with yogurt.
tips:
  - Do not rush the onion – twenty minutes over low heat make all the difference.
  - "With ready-made filo: alternate two sheets brushed with oil with the filling."
mistakes:
  - Burnt onion – the whole banitsa turns bitter.
  - Eggs in a hot filling – they curdle.
substitutions:
  - Leeks instead of onion – a milder, sweeter flavour.
  - A handful of crumbled cheese in the filling, if you are not making it meat- and dairy-free.
plating: A triangular piece with a spoonful of yogurt and a few rings of spring onion.
serving: Warm, with yogurt or ayran.
storage: Up to 2 days in the refrigerator. Reheat in the oven.
