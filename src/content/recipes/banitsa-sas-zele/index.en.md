mise:
  - Cut the cabbage into very thin strips and the onion finely.
  - Take the filo out of the refrigerator 30 minutes ahead.
  - Grease a tin with oil.
technique: The cabbage is salted and squeezed before it is braised – this way it releases its water at the start and the banitsa does not turn out wet. The filling is cooled before the eggs go in and before rolling.
temperature: The cabbage is braised for 20–25 minutes over medium heat. The banitsa is baked at 190 °C (170 °C fan) for 40–45 minutes.
balance: The cabbage is sweet, the onion deepens the flavour, and the black pepper is the only sharp accent.

steps:
  - title: Soften the cabbage
    text: Salt the shredded cabbage and leave it for 15 minutes. Squeeze it out well with your hands.
    why: The salt draws out the water; squeezed cabbage braises instead of boiling.
    time: 15
  - title: Braise the filling
    text: Heat 100 ml of oil in a wide frying pan, sweat the onion for 4–5 minutes and add the cabbage. Cook for 20–25 minutes over medium heat, stirring, until it is tender and slightly coloured. Off the heat, add the paprika and the black pepper.
    cue: The cabbage is soft and golden, and there is no liquid in the pan.
    avoid: Paprika burns quickly – it goes in at the end, off the heat.
    time: 25
  - title: Cool and bind
    text: Let the filling cool to lukewarm and stir the eggs into it.
    avoid: In a hot filling the eggs curdle.
  - title: Roll the banitsa
    text: Brush each sheet with oil, spread the filling in a strip and roll into a loose roll. Arrange the rolls in a spiral or side by side in the tin and brush the surface with oil.
  - title: Bake
    text: Bake for 40–45 minutes at 190 °C, until the banitsa is dark golden and crisp.
    time: 45
  - title: Let it rest
    text: Cover with a cloth for 10 minutes, cut and serve.
    time: 10
tips:
  - It can also be made with sauerkraut rinsed of its brine – then use less salt.
  - The filling can be made in advance and kept in the refrigerator until the next day.
mistakes:
  - Unsqueezed cabbage – the banitsa goes soft underneath.
  - Burnt cabbage – the banitsa turns bitter.
substitutions:
  - Leeks instead of part of the onion for a milder flavour.
  - Without eggs, for a lenten (fasting) version – add a little more oil.
plating: A piece with visible layers, a sprig of parsley on the side and a small bowl of yogurt.
serving: Warm, with yogurt or ayran.
storage: Up to 2 days in the refrigerator. Reheat in the oven.
allergens:
