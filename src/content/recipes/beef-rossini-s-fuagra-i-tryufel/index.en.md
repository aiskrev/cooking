mise:
  - Take the medallions out 30 minutes ahead. Pat them dry and tie them with string so that they keep their shape.
  - Cut the brioche and fry it in a pan with butter until golden and crisp. Leave on a rack.
  - Keep the foie gras chilled until the moment of searing – cold, it slices and sears more easily.
  - Slice the truffle into very thin shavings with a mandoline or a sharp knife.
technique: Searing the meat with butter and thyme, a very brief sear of the foie gras in a dry pan, simmering a sauce with Madeira, building the dish upwards.
temperature: "The meat is seared for 2–3 minutes on each side over high heat and then rested; the core at removal is 50 °C, and 54–56 °C after the rest (medium rare). The foie gras goes in a dry pan over very high heat: 30–40 seconds per side."
balance: Foie gras is sweet and rich, truffle is earthy, and the wine and Madeira are acidic and aromatic. Do not add more fat than necessary.

tips:
  - The brioche must be crisp; if it softens, the juices make it soggy.
  - Do not unpack the foie gras until the very moment of searing.
  - The sauce can be made ahead and warmed gently.
mistakes:
  - A pan that is not hot enough – the foie gras melts instead of searing.
  - Overheated foie gras – only fat is left.
substitutions:
  - Instead of foie gras – a mushroom and nut pâté or seared porcini.
  - Instead of brioche – crisp toast made from white bread.
plating: Brioche – medallion – foie gras – shavings of truffle; the sauce to the side and a little over the top. Built upwards, on a dark plate.
serving: Immediately, with potato or celeriac purée and a glass of Bordeaux.
storage: Best straight away. The sauce keeps for 2 days in the refrigerator.
allergens:
