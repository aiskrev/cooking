mise:
  - Heat the oven to 190 °C.
  - Wash the beetroots without cutting off the roots and tails.
  - Toast the caraway in a dry pan until fragrant and crush it lightly.
  - Take the goat cheese out of the refrigerator 20 minutes ahead.
technique: Baking in a salt-dough crust, reducing beetroot juice to a glaze, a creamy goat-cheese emulsion and toasted spices.
temperature: The beetroot is baked in the crust at 190 °C for 75–90 minutes depending on size, until a skewer goes in freely. The sauce is reduced over medium-high heat to one fifth of its volume. The dish is served at room temperature.
balance: The crust does not make the beetroot salty, but its flavour is concentrated, so salt only at the end with flaky salt. Vinegar and cherries bring acidity, honey in the sauce roundness and the goat curd softness.

steps:
  - title: Make the salt crust
    text: Mix the flour, salt, egg whites and water into a firm, pliable dough. Roll it out to 5 mm.
    time: 10
  - title: Bake the beetroot
    text: Arrange the beetroots with the thyme on the dough and wrap them tightly. Bake for 75–90 minutes at 190 °C. Leave to rest for 20 minutes, crack the crust and peel the beetroot.
    why: The crust traps the steam – the beetroot bakes in its own juices.
    cue: A skewer goes into the beetroot without resistance.
    time: 90
  - title: Make the jus
    text: Boil the beetroot juice with the vinegar and honey and reduce to about 100 ml. Whisk in the cold butter off the heat.
    cue: The jus is glossy, ruby-red and coats the spoon.
    time: 25
  - title: Whip the goat cheese
    text: Whip the goat cheese with the crème fraîche, lemon juice and salt into a smooth cream.
    time: 3
  - title: Cut the beetroot
    text: Cut the beetroot into wedges and halves so that the concentric rings show. Season with a drop of olive oil and flaky salt.
    cue: The cut face shows clear rings.
  - title: Plate
    text: Pour a spoonful of jus onto the plate. Arrange the beetroot at different heights, add quenelles of goat curd, the cherries and the leaves. Sprinkle with caraway.

tips:
  - Different beetroot varieties (red, golden, candy-striped) make the plate richer.
  - The salt crust is discarded – it is only an "oven" for the beetroot.
  - The jus can be made 2 days ahead.
mistakes:
  - Cutting off the roots before baking – the beetroot loses juice and colour.
  - Underbaked beetroot – hard and earthy.
  - Oversalting at the end – the beetroot is already concentrated.
substitutions:
  - Instead of goat cheese – curd cheese whipped with cream.
  - Instead of sour cherries – cranberries.
  - Instead of a salt crust – baking in foil (a less concentrated flavour).
plating: "A pale plate with a wide rim: a pool of ruby jus, beetroot at different heights, three quenelles of goat curd, cherries and leaves, caraway on top."
serving: "A starter or vegetarian main course at room temperature. Good non-alcoholic pairings: sour-cherry juice topped with sparkling water, or chilled rosehip tea."
storage: The baked beetroot keeps in the refrigerator for up to 3 days; the jus for up to 5 days.
