mise:
  - Take the filo out of the refrigerator 30 minutes ahead – cold sheets break when unfolded.
  - Melt half of the butter and let it cool slightly; you will need the other half at the end.
  - Crumble the cheese with a fork into coarse crumbs and grease a round tin about 28 cm in diameter with butter.
technique: Each sheet is brushed thinly with butter and rolled loosely around the filling. The butter separates the layers, and the air in the roll lets the banitsa rise instead of settling.
temperature: Oven at 190 °C (170 °C fan), middle shelf, 40–45 minutes. If the top darkens before the end, cover it loosely with baking paper.
balance: The salt comes from the cheese, so the filling is not salted further. The yogurt and the bicarbonate of soda make it light, and the butter gives the aroma and the crust.

steps:
  - title: Prepare the ingredients
    text: Lay out the filo, the cheese, the eggs, the yogurt and the butter on the work surface. Switch the oven on to 190 °C.
    why: Working with filo does not tolerate interruptions – left in the air, it dries out within minutes.
    photo: 01-banitsa-stapka-1.webp
  - title: Mix the filling
    text: Stir the bicarbonate of soda into the yogurt and wait for it to fizz. Beat the eggs with a fork, add the yogurt, the crumbled cheese and a little black pepper. Stir only until the ingredients are combined.
    why: The soda reacts with the acidity of the yogurt and the filling becomes lighter.
    cue: The mixture is thick, with visible pieces of cheese.
    avoid: Do not purée the cheese – the filling loses its structure and turns sticky.
    photo: 02-banitsa-stapka-2.webp
  - title: Brush the sheet with butter
    text: Spread out one sheet on the work surface and brush it thinly with melted butter using a brush. Keep the remaining sheets covered with a clean, slightly damp cloth.
    why: The thin layer of butter is what later separates the banitsa into crisp layers.
    avoid: Do not overdo the butter – the sheet should glisten, not swim.
    photo: 03-banitsa-stapka-3.webp
  - title: Add the filling and roll
    text: Spread 2–3 tablespoons of filling in a strip along the long side of the sheet and roll it into a loose roll, gently gathering it with your fingers like an accordion.
    cue: The roll is evenly thick and not torn.
    avoid: If you roll tightly, the filling has no room to swell and the sheets crack.
    photo: 04-banitsa-stapka-4.webp
  - title: Shape the spiral
    text: Coil the first roll like a snail in the middle of the tin. Repeat with the remaining sheets and filling, placing each next roll against the end of the previous one until the tin is full.
    photo: 05-banitsa-stapka-5.webp
  - title: Brush with butter
    text: Heat the remaining butter until it begins to foam and brush the whole surface with it. If any filling is left, spoon it between the coils.
    why: The butter on top gives the golden colour and the crisp crust.
    photo: 06-banitsa-stapka-6.webp
  - title: Bake
    text: Bake for 40–45 minutes on the middle shelf of the oven, until the banitsa has risen and is a dark golden colour.
    cue: The crust is evenly golden and sounds hollow when tapped lightly.
    time: 45
    photo: 07-banitsa-stapka-7.webp
  - title: Let it rest
    text: Take the banitsa out, sprinkle it with a few drops of cold water and cover it with a cloth for 10 minutes. Then cut it with a sharp knife.
    why: Under the cloth the crust softens just slightly and does not shatter when cut, and the filling sets.
    time: 10
    photo: 08-banitsa-stapka-8.webp
  - title: Serve
    text: Put a piece of banitsa on a plate, add a spoonful of cold yogurt and sprinkle with fresh herbs.
    photo: 09-banitsa-stapka-9.webp
tips:
  - For a crisper base, grease the tin generously with butter and move it to a lower shelf for the last 10 minutes.
  - If the sheets tear, overlap two halves and roll them as one – in the spiral it does not show.
  - Banitsa left over from the previous day comes back to life in 8–10 minutes in an oven at 170 °C.
mistakes:
  - Dried-out sheets – cover them with a cloth while you work.
  - Too much filling in one roll – the sheet tears and the filling leaks into the tin.
  - Cutting straight after baking – the layers are crushed and the cheese runs out.
substitutions:
  - Part of the cheese can be replaced with izvara (fresh curd cheese) for a milder flavour; then add a pinch of salt.
  - Half of the butter can be replaced with sunflower oil – the crust is crisper, but the aroma is weaker.
plating: A piece with clearly visible layers, a spoonful of yogurt on the side and a few fresh herb leaves.
serving: Warm, with ayran, boza or yogurt.
storage: Up to 2 days in the refrigerator, covered. Reheat in the oven, not in the microwave – there the crust turns soft.
allergens:
