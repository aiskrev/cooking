mise:
  - The day before, soak the sweetbreads in cold water in the refrigerator for 12 hours, changing the water 2–3 times until it runs clear.
  - If the morels are dried, soak them for 30 minutes in warm water; strain the water through a cloth and keep it for the sauce.
  - Take the fillet out of the refrigerator 30 minutes before searing and pat it dry.
  - Warm the plates – this dish cools quickly because the portions are small.
technique: Blanching and pressing sweetbreads, searing and butter-basting, roasting a fillet to pink, a sweet pea purée, sautéing morels, reducing a roast-veal jus and mounting it with cold butter.
temperature: The sweetbreads are blanched at a gentle simmer (about 90 °C) for 4–5 minutes. The fillet is seared over high heat and finished in the oven at 180 °C to 52–54 °C at the core; after resting it is about 56 °C (pink). The sweetbreads are seared over medium-high heat until the crust is golden and the core is about 65 °C. The sauce is finished below the boil, at 70–80 °C, when the butter is whisked in.
balance: The fillet and sweetbreads are soft and rich, so the peas and mint bring freshness, the wine in the sauce acidity and the morels depth. Salt with care, because the sauce is concentrated.

steps:
  - title: Lay out the ingredients
    text: Arrange the fillet, soaked sweetbreads, peas, morels, butter, shallot, thyme, wine and stock. Set aside the larger pea pods for garnish.
    photo: 01-teleshko-stapka-1.webp
  - title: Blanch and clean the sweetbreads
    text: Put the sweetbreads in cold water with the milk, bay leaf and salt and bring to a gentle simmer. Cook for 4–5 minutes, lift into iced water and peel off the thin membrane and sinews. Press them between two trays under a weight for 1 hour in the refrigerator, then cut into 50–60 g medallions.
    why: Pressing makes the sweetbreads dense and even, so they sear evenly.
    cue: The sweetbreads are white, firm and free of membrane.
    time: 5
    photo: 02-teleshko-stapka-2.webp
  - title: Make the pea purée
    text: Cook the peas in salted water for 3–4 minutes and chill them at once in iced water. Blend with the butter, warmed cream and mint until smooth, pass through a sieve and keep warm.
    cue: The purée is bright green and smooth, without skins.
    avoid: Do not keep the purée on the heat for long – it loses its colour.
    time: 5
    photo: 03-teleshko-stapka-3.webp
  - title: Sauté the morels
    text: Halve the morels lengthways and rinse them quickly to remove any sand. Sweat the shallot in the butter, add the morels and cook for 4–5 minutes. Add the wine and fresh peas and cook for another minute. Season.
    cue: The morels are tender and glossy and the liquid has almost evaporated.
    time: 6
  - title: Make the sauce
    text: Sweat the shallots with a little butter, pour in the wine and reduce almost completely. Add the stock, thyme and the strained morel water and reduce by half. Strain.
    cue: The sauce coats the spoon and has a deep brown shine.
    time: 20
  - title: Sear and roast the fillet
    text: Season the fillet with salt and pepper. Sear it in a hot pan with the oil on all sides until deep golden, add half the butter and thyme and baste several times. Finish in the oven at 180 °C for 8–12 minutes to 52–54 °C at the core. Rest for 10 minutes.
    cue: The thermometer reads 52–54 °C; the meat is springy when pressed.
    time: 15
    photo: 04-teleshko-stapka-6.webp
  - title: Sear the sweetbreads
    text: Pat the medallions dry, season and sear them in a pan with a little oil over medium-high heat for 3 minutes per side until a deep golden crust forms.
    cue: The crust is even and crisp.
    time: 6
    photo: 05-teleshko-stapka-7.webp
  - title: Baste with butter and thyme
    text: Add the remaining butter, the garlic and thyme. Tilt the pan and baste the sweetbreads with the foaming butter for 1–2 minutes. Lift onto paper.
    why: The foam carries the thyme aroma and finishes the crust.
    time: 2
    photo: 06-teleshko-stapka-8.webp
  - title: Finish the sauce and plate
    text: Warm the sauce and whisk in the cold butter off the heat. Put a spoonful of pea purée on each warm plate, slice the fillet thickly and arrange it next to the sweetbread. Add the morels and peas and pour the sauce around the meat.
    cue: The sauce is glossy and does not split.
    photo: 07-teleshko-stapka-9.webp
tips:
  - The sweetbreads can be blanched and pressed a day ahead; sear them just before serving.
  - If you have no fresh morels, dried ones give an even more concentrated aroma – keep the soaking water.
  - Slice the fillet only after resting, so the juices stay in.
mistakes:
  - Membranes left on the sweetbreads – they shrink and make them chewy.
  - Too low a heat when searing – the sweetbreads release water and never crust.
  - Sand in the morels – rinse them quickly but thoroughly.
substitutions:
  - Instead of sweetbreads – thick medallions of pre-braised veal cheek.
  - Instead of morels – porcini or oyster mushrooms.
  - Instead of cream in the purée – a little more butter and cooking water.
plating: "A white or light plate: an oval of pea purée on the left, two thick slices of fillet and a sweetbread medallion in the centre, morels and peas around them, sauce around the meat without covering the crust."
serving: Straight away, with a white Burgundy or a light red wine (Pinot Noir).
storage: Best eaten at once. The sauce and purée keep for up to 2 days in the refrigerator; seared sweetbreads lose their crispness.
