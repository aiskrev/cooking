mise:
  - "Joint the guinea fowl: remove the breasts with the skin on and the legs; chop the backbone and wings for the sauce."
  - Pat the breasts dry and leave them uncovered in the refrigerator – drier skin crisps better.
  - Peel the pears and cut into eighths; separate the cabbage into leaves and cut out the thick ribs.
technique: Jointing a bird, slow-braising the legs, roasting bones for a dark sauce, searing breasts skin-side down and butter-basting, chestnut purée, a crisp crumble, caramelising pears and blanching cabbage.
temperature: The legs are braised in the oven at 150 °C for about 1 hour 30 minutes, until the meat begins to come away from the bone. The bones are roasted at 210 °C for 30–35 minutes. The breasts are seared skin-side down over medium-high heat for 6–7 minutes, turned and finished to 64–65 °C at the core; after resting they reach about 67 °C and stay juicy.
balance: Chestnuts are sweet and earthy, so the pear and the wine in the sauce bring acidity and the cabbage a slight bitterness. The crumble adds the texture the soft elements lack.

steps:
  - title: Lay out the ingredients
    text: Arrange the guinea fowl, chestnuts, pears, cabbage, butter, stock and herbs. Check that the chestnuts are fully peeled of their inner skin.
    photo: 01-tokachka-stapka-1.webp
  - title: Joint the birds
    text: Remove the breasts with the skin on and the legs at the joint. Clean the thigh bone for a neater look. Chop the backbone and wings for the sauce.
    photo: 02-tokachka-stapka-2.webp
  - title: Braise the legs
    text: Season the legs, brown them in a pot, add the vegetables, wine, stock and bay leaf. Cover and braise in the oven at 150 °C for about 1 hour 30 minutes. Lift out the legs, strain the liquid and keep it.
    cue: The meat is tender and comes away easily from the bone.
    time: 90
    photo: 03-tokachka-stapka-3.webp
  - title: Make the sauce
    text: Roast the chopped bones until deep golden. Transfer them to a pot, sweat the shallot, deglaze with the wine, add the stock, the braising liquid and thyme and reduce for about 40 minutes to a thick sauce. Strain.
    cue: The sauce is dark, glossy and coats the spoon.
    time: 45
    photo: 04-tokachka-stapka-4.webp
  - title: Make the chestnut purée
    text: Simmer the chestnuts in the milk for 15 minutes and blend with the butter to a velvety purée; season. If too thick, add more milk.
    cue: The purée falls slowly from the spoon and has no lumps.
    time: 15
    photo: 05-tokachka-stapka-5.webp
  - title: Prepare the pears and cabbage
    text: Sear the pears in the butter and honey for 2 minutes on each side. Blanch the cabbage leaves in salted water for 2 minutes, chill them and toss in the butter just before serving.
    cue: The pears are golden but hold their shape; the cabbage is bright green.
    time: 8
    photo: 06-tokachka-stapka-6.webp
  - title: Make the crumble
    text: Crush the chestnuts and hazelnuts finely, mix with the breadcrumbs and fry in the butter until golden. Cool on paper.
    time: 5
    photo: 07-tokachka-stapka-7.webp
  - title: Sear the breasts
    text: Season the breasts and place them skin-side down in a cold pan with the oil. Bring to medium-high heat and sear for 6–7 minutes until the skin is crisp. Turn, add the butter, garlic and thyme and baste for 2–3 minutes to 64–65 °C at the core.
    why: A cold pan renders the fat under the skin slowly, so it crisps without burning.
    cue: The skin is golden and dry and the core reads 64–65 °C.
    time: 10
    photo: 08-tokachka-stapka-8.webp
  - title: Glaze the legs and finish the sauce
    text: Warm the legs in a little of the sauce and baste them several times until coated in a glossy glaze. Whisk the cold butter into the remaining sauce off the heat.
    photo: 09-tokachka-stapka-9.webp
  - title: Plate
    text: Halve the breasts lengthways and sprinkle with crumble. Swipe an arc of chestnut purée, add the breast and leg, the pear, cabbage and whole chestnuts. Pour the sauce around the meat and garnish with small leaves.
    photo: 10-tokachka-stapka-10.webp
tips:
  - The legs and sauce can be made a day ahead – they even improve.
  - Dry the breasts uncovered in the refrigerator for at least 2 hours for crisper skin.
  - If the crumble softens, re-toast it for a minute in a dry pan.
mistakes:
  - Overcooked breasts – guinea fowl is lean and dries out above 70 °C.
  - Purée made from unpeeled chestnuts – the skin is bitter and makes the texture gritty.
  - Too much sauce on the plate – the crumble goes soft.
substitutions:
  - Instead of guinea fowl – corn-fed chicken or pheasant (shorter time for the breasts).
  - Instead of hazelnuts in the crumble – walnuts or almonds.
  - Instead of savoy cabbage – Brussels sprout leaves or kale.
plating: "A wide light ceramic plate: an arc of chestnut purée, the crumble-topped breast on the right, the leg behind, pears and cabbage between them, whole chestnuts and sauce around the meat."
serving: Straight away, with an oaked white wine (Chardonnay) or a light red.
storage: The legs and sauce keep for 3 days in the refrigerator; the breasts are seared just before serving.
