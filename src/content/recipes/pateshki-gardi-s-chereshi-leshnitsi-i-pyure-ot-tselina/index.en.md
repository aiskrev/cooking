mise:
  - Score the duck skin in a crosshatch with a sharp knife without cutting the meat, and pat it dry.
  - Peel and dice the celeriac.
  - Halve the endives lengthways.
technique: A cold start for the duck breasts and slow rendering of the fat; resting; a celeriac purée; a wine and stock reduction with cherries, finished with butter; caramelised endive.
temperature: The breasts go into a cold pan skin side down and cook over a medium-low heat for 10–12 minutes, then 2–3 minutes on the other side – to 55–57 °C in the centre. They rest for 8 minutes.
balance: The acidity of the cherries and vinegar cuts through the richness of the duck. Celeriac brings softness, endive a light bitterness, hazelnuts crunch.

steps:
  - title: Cook the celeriac purée
    text: Simmer the celeriac in the milk over a low heat for 20 minutes. Blend with the butter until smooth and season with salt.
    time: 20
  - title: Sear the duck
    text: Season the breasts and lay them skin side down in a cold pan. Cook over a medium-low heat for 10–12 minutes, pouring off the excess fat. Turn for 2–3 minutes.
    why: A cold start renders the fat gradually and makes the skin thin and crisp.
    cue: The skin is deep golden and crisp and the meat feels springy.
    time: 14
  - title: Rest
    text: Set the breasts skin side up on a rack with the thyme and rest for 8 minutes.
    time: 8
  - title: Make the sauce
    text: Pour the fat out of the pan, add the wine and reduce by half. Add the stock, sugar and half the cherries and simmer to a syrupy consistency. Strain, add the remaining cherries and the vinegar and stir in the cold butter.
  - title: Caramelise the endive
    text: Sear the endive halves cut side down in the butter for 4–5 minutes until golden.
  - title: Slice and plate
    text: Cut the breasts into thick slices. Spoon purée onto the plate, top with the duck and endive, pour over the sauce and cherries and scatter with hazelnuts and microgreens.
tips:
  - The rendered duck fat is excellent for roast potatoes.
  - Out of season, use frozen cherries without thawing.
  - If the breasts are thick, finish them for 3–4 minutes in the oven at 180 °C.
mistakes:
  - A hot pan at the start – the skin burns outside while the fat remains.
  - Slicing without resting – the juices run out.
  - Cooking the cherries too long – they turn mushy.
substitutions:
  - Instead of cherries – sour cherries (with a little more sugar) or plums.
  - Instead of celeriac – parsnip.

storage: The duck is best at once. The sauce and purée keep in the refrigerator for up to 2 days.
