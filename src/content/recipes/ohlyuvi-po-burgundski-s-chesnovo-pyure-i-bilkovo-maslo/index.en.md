mise:
  - If the snails are tinned, rinse them and dry them on paper.
  - Finely chop the shallots and garlic, and the parsley.
  - Cut the brioche into 1 cm cubes.
technique: Roasting whole heads of garlic and a purée with cream; snails cooked in butter with aromatics; brown butter; toasted brioche.
temperature: The garlic roasts wrapped in foil at 180 °C for 40–45 minutes. The snails cook over a medium heat for 4–5 minutes – just to warm through and take on the aroma. The brioche is toasted in butter until golden.
balance: Butter and garlic are rich, so the lemon and wine must come through. Roasted garlic is sweet, raw garlic in the butter is sharp; together they give depth.

steps:
  - title: Lay out the ingredients
    text: Lay out the snails, butter, shallots, garlic, lemon, parsley and brioche.
  - title: Prepare the snails
    text: Check the snails and remove any remaining bits of shell. Measure out the wine and butter.
  - title: Dry
    text: Dry the snails carefully with kitchen paper – moisture stops the butter from coating them.
  - title: Make the purée and brioche
    text: Roast the garlic wrapped in foil with the olive oil for 40–45 minutes at 180 °C. Squeeze out the cloves, blend with the warmed cream and season with salt. Toast the brioche in the butter until golden.
    time: 45
  - title: Make the brown butter
    text: Cook the butter over a medium heat until it smells nutty and the solids turn golden brown.
  - title: Cook the snails
    text: Melt the butter, soften the shallots and garlic for 2 minutes, add the snails and wine and cook for 4–5 minutes. Add the parsley and lemon juice and season with salt.
    avoid: Do not cook the snails for long – they turn tough.
    time: 5
tips:
  - Ready-prepared tinned or frozen snails are the easiest choice; live ones need days of purging.
  - The roasted garlic purée keeps in the refrigerator for up to 4 days.
  - Serve with extra brioche for mopping up.
mistakes:
  - Wet snails – the butter splits.
  - Burnt garlic in the butter – it turns bitter.
  - Brioche added too early – it goes soft.
substitutions:
  - Instead of snails – button mushrooms cooked the same way (vegetarian).
  - Instead of brioche – baguette.

serving: "Hot, as a starter. Suitable non-alcoholic pairings: sparkling water with lemon or alcohol-free apple cider."
storage: Cook the snails just before serving. The purée and herb oil can be made a day ahead.
