mise:
  - Beat the eggs with the milk and the salt.
  - Chop the walnuts coarsely; you can toast them for 5 minutes in a dry pan for a stronger aroma.
  - Prepare a plate and a paper towel on which to drain the crêpes.
technique: The batter is whisked smooth without lumps and rests for half an hour so that the flour swells. The sparkling water makes it lighter, and the thin layer in the pan makes the edge lacy.
temperature: A pan 22–24 cm in diameter is heated over medium to high heat. A crêpe cooks for about 1 minute on the first side and 30 seconds on the second.
balance: The crêpe is almost neutral – the flavour comes from the honey, the walnuts and the ice cream, so the honey should not be too sweet and the walnuts should be slightly bitter.

steps:
  - title: Mix the batter
    text: Beat the eggs with the milk, the sugar and the salt. Add the flour in parts and whisk until there are no lumps. Finally add the sparkling water and the oil.
    cue: The batter is as thin as pouring cream and coats the back of the spoon.
  - title: Let it rest
    text: Cover the bowl and leave it for 30 minutes at room temperature.
    why: The flour absorbs the liquid and the crêpes do not tear when turned.
    time: 30
  - title: Heat the pan
    text: Heat the pan over medium to high heat and grease it very thinly with oil, using a brush or a piece of paper.
  - title: Cook the crêpes
    text: Pour in about half a ladleful of batter and swirl the pan to spread it into a thin layer. Cook for about 1 minute, until the edges begin to lift, turn and cook for another 30 seconds. Repeat with the remaining batter.
    cue: The surface is matt and the edges have golden lace.
    avoid: If the pan smokes, take it off the heat for half a minute – the first crêpe is sacrificed.
    time: 20
  - title: Fold and serve
    text: Fold each crêpe in half and then once more. Arrange two or three on a plate, sprinkle with the walnuts, drizzle with honey and put ice cream on top.
tips:
  - The crêpes can be cooked in advance and reheated in a pan for 20 seconds on each side.
  - The first crêpe almost always fails – the pan is not yet stable in temperature.
mistakes:
  - Lumps in the batter – strain it through a sieve.
  - A thick layer of batter – the crêpe turns out like a flatbread.
substitutions:
  - Plant milk and oil instead of butter – for a lenten version without eggs, use more sparkling water.
  - Jam or chocolate spread instead of honey.
plating: Two or three folded crêpes, a handful of walnuts, honey in a thin stream and a scoop of ice cream on the side.
serving: Warm, with coffee or tea.
storage: Cooked crêpes keep for up to 2 days in the refrigerator, wrapped in cling film, or up to a month in the freezer.
