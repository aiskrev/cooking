mise:
  - Clean the red mullet, fillet them and remove the pin bones with tweezers. Keep the heads and bones, without the gills.
  - Soak saffron for the sauce and for the rouille separately in a spoonful of hot water.
  - Slice the potatoes very thinly (mandoline) and soak them in cold water.
technique: Filleting small fish, a stock of roasted bones and shellfish, reducing it to a sauce, a mayonnaise with saffron and potato (rouille), frying thin potato wafers, searing fillets skin-side down.
temperature: The stock simmers for 30 minutes and is reduced by half. The potato wafers are fried at 160–165 °C until golden. The fillets are seared skin-side down over medium-high heat for 2 minutes, then 20–30 seconds on the other side.
balance: Bouillabaisse is intense and iodine-rich, so orange peel and fennel freshen it, and the rouille adds a garlicky bite. Crisp potatoes balance the soft textures.

steps:
  - title: Lay out the ingredients
    text: Arrange the red mullet, the prawns or mussels, fennel, tomatoes, orange, saffron, potatoes and garlic.
  - title: Fillet the fish
    text: Remove the fillets with a thin knife, take out the pin bones down the middle with tweezers and pat the skin dry. Keep in the refrigerator.
    why: Red mullet has fine bones you can feel – tweezers are essential.
  - title: Make the stock
    text: Sear the bones and heads in the olive oil, add the prawns, onion, fennel, garlic and tomato purée and sweat for 5 minutes. Deglaze with the pastis, add the tomatoes, orange peel, saffron and water. Simmer for 30 minutes.
    cue: The stock is deep orange and smells of the sea and fennel.
    time: 35
  - title: Strain and reduce
    text: Press the solids in a colander to release all the flavour and strain the stock through a fine sieve. Reduce by half and season.
    cue: The sauce is clear, glossy and full-flavoured.
    time: 15
  - title: Finish the sauce
    text: Strain once more into a bowl. Keep warm.
  - title: Make the rouille
    text: Blend the potato, yolk, garlic, saffron and lemon juice. Add the oil and olive oil in a thin stream until it becomes a thick mayonnaise. Season.
    cue: The rouille is thick, orange-yellow and glossy.
  - title: Fry the potatoes
    text: Pat the potato slices dry and fry them in batches at 160–165 °C until golden. Salt at once.
    cue: The wafers are golden and translucent at the edges.
    time: 10
  - title: Braise the fennel
    text: Cut the fennel into wedges, sear them in the butter, pour in the stock and braise for 10–12 minutes.
    time: 12
  - title: Sear the red mullet
    text: Salt the fillets and place them skin-side down in a hot pan with olive oil. Press lightly with a spatula for the first few seconds and fry for 2 minutes. Add the butter, turn and cook for another 20–30 seconds.
    cue: The skin is crisp and red-gold and the flesh just opaque.
    avoid: Without pressing, the fillets curl and the skin does not crisp evenly.
    time: 3
  - title: Baste and serve
    text: Baste the fillets with the foaming butter. Arrange the fennel, two fillets, a spoonful of rouille and the potato wafers, and pour the sauce around the fish.
tips:
  - The stock can be made a day ahead; the fish is cooked at the last moment.
  - If the rouille splits, start again with a fresh yolk and add the split mixture drop by drop.
mistakes:
  - Gills in the stock – they make it bitter.
  - Damp skin – it does not crisp and sticks to the pan.
substitutions:
  - Instead of red mullet – gilt-head bream or sea bass (longer searing).
  - Instead of pastis – white wine and a pinch of aniseed.
plating: "A wide light plate: two fillets skin-side up on braised fennel, a quenelle of rouille, potato wafers and sauce around the fish."
serving: With a white wine from Provence or a rosé.
storage: The stock keeps for 3 days, the rouille for 1 day in the refrigerator. The fish is cooked just before serving.
