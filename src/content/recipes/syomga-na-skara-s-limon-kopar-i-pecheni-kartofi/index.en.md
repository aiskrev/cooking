mise:
  - Take the salmon out of the refrigerator 15 minutes before cooking and pat it dry with kitchen paper.
  - Halve the potatoes and dry them.
  - Snap off the woody ends of the asparagus.
technique: Oven-roast potatoes; salmon seared in a very hot grill pan, skin side first; quickly seared asparagus; a butter sauce with lemon and dill.
temperature: The potatoes roast at 220 °C for 30–35 minutes. The salmon cooks for 4 minutes skin side down and 1–2 minutes on the other side, to 50–52 °C in the centre for a juicy, slightly translucent middle.
balance: Oily fish needs acidity – the lemon and dill butter provide it. Roast potatoes bring crunch, the tomatoes sweetness.

steps:
  - title: Roast the potatoes
    text: Toss the potatoes with olive oil, salt and crushed garlic and roast at 220 °C for 30–35 minutes, turning once.
    time: 35
  - title: Prepare the salmon
    text: Pat the fillets dry, brush with olive oil and season with salt and pepper.
  - title: Sear the salmon
    text: Heat a grill pan until very hot. Lay the fillets skin side down and leave them for 4 minutes. Turn and cook for another 1–2 minutes.
    cue: The fish releases from the pan by itself; the sides are opaque almost to the top.
    avoid: Do not move the fish before the crust forms – it will tear.
    time: 6
  - title: Sear the asparagus and tomatoes
    text: In the same pan, sear the asparagus for 3–4 minutes, add the tomatoes for the last minute and season with salt.
  - title: Make the dill butter
    text: Melt the butter, add the lemon juice and dill and stir.
  - title: Serve
    text: Arrange the potatoes and asparagus, place the salmon on top, spoon over the butter and sprinkle with parsley and dill. Add lemon slices.
tips:
  - The skin only crisps if it is dry – pat it carefully.
  - If the fillet is thick, finish it in the oven for 3 minutes.
  - Instead of potatoes – mashed potato, as in some of the photographs.
mistakes:
  - A pan that is not hot enough – the fish sticks.
  - Overcooking – the salmon dries out and leaks white albumin.
  - Lemon juice on raw fish – it "cooks" the surface.
substitutions:
  - Instead of salmon – trout or mackerel.
  - Instead of asparagus – green beans.
plating: "A large plate: potatoes and asparagus, the salmon on top, the dill butter, lemon slices and tomatoes."
serving: "At once, while the skin is crisp. Suitable non-alcoholic pairings: mint lemonade or cold green tea."
storage: Leftovers keep in the refrigerator for up to 1 day; the salmon is also good cold in a salad.
