mise:
  - The mince must be cold, straight from the refrigerator – warm fat smears and the kebapcheta turn out dry.
  - Light the charcoal at least 40 minutes before cooking.
technique: Long kneading with salt draws the proteins out of the meat and they bind the mince – that is why the kebapche holds together without egg or bread. The sparkling water and the soda make it juicier and fluffier.
temperature: "Strong, even embers without flames: a hand can stay 10 cm above the grill for no more than 3 seconds. Cooking time 8–10 minutes in total. Minced meat is cooked all the way through – 71 °C in the middle, with no pink."
balance: The cumin should be noticeable without overpowering the meat. The lyutenitsa and the raw onion add a sweetness and sharpness that the meat does not have.

steps:
  - title: Prepare the ingredients
    text: Lay out the mince, the spices and the sparkling water. Cut the onion for serving and prepare the lyutenitsa and the roasted peppers.
    photo: 01-kebapcheta-stapka-1.webp
  - title: Season the mince
    text: Sprinkle the mince with the salt, the black pepper, the cumin and the soda dissolved in the sparkling water.
    photo: 02-kebapcheta-stapka-2.webp
  - title: Knead
    text: Knead by hand for 8–10 minutes, pressing and turning the mince like dough.
    why: In kneading, the salt draws out the proteins and the mince becomes sticky – this is what holds it together on the grill.
    cue: The mixture is smooth, clings to the palm and pulls into threads.
    time: 10
    photo: 03-kebapcheta-stapka-3.webp
  - title: Shape
    text: With wet hands shape kebapcheta 10–12 cm long and about 2.5 cm thick – 60–70 g each. You will get 10–11 of them.
    cue: The kebapcheta are smooth, without cracks on the surface.
    photo: 04-kebapcheta-stapka-4.webp
  - title: Chill
    text: Arrange them in a tray, cover them and leave them in the refrigerator for at least 2 hours, and ideally overnight.
    why: In the cold the spices penetrate the meat, and the kebapcheta firm up and do not fall apart when turned.
    photo: 05-kebapcheta-stapka-5.webp
  - title: Prepare the embers
    text: Wait for the flames to die down and for the coals to be covered with a thin layer of grey ash.
    cue: The embers glow evenly and there is no open flame.
    avoid: Over a flame the kebapcheta char on the outside and stay raw inside.
    photo: 06-kebapcheta-stapka-6.webp
  - title: Heat the grill
    text: Put the grate over the embers for 5 minutes, clean it with a brush and grease it with oil using a piece of paper or half an onion.
    why: Meat does not stick to a hot, oiled grate.
    time: 5
    photo: 07-kebapcheta-stapka-7.webp
  - title: Grill
    text: Lay the kebapcheta across the grate and cook them for 8–10 minutes in total, turning them every 2–3 minutes.
    cue: The kebapcheta are brown, with clear grill marks, and spring back under pressure; there is no pink in the middle.
    avoid: Do not press them with the tongs – the juice runs onto the embers.
    time: 10
    photo: 08-kebapcheta-stapka-8.webp
  - title: Serve
    text: Serve immediately, with lyutenitsa, roasted peppers and finely chopped onion sprinkled with parsley.
    photo: 09-kebapcheta-stapka-9.webp
tips:
  - Fat carries the flavour – with lean mince the kebapcheta turn out dry and crumbly.
  - If you have no charcoal grill, use a well-heated ridged grill pan.
  - Grill one test kebapche first and taste it for salt.
mistakes:
  - Short kneading – the kebapcheta crack and fall apart on the grill.
  - Turning before a crust has formed – they stick and tear.
  - Leaving them on the grill "just in case" – they dry out.
substitutions:
  - Only pork mince – fattier and softer; only beef – drier, so add a spoonful of oil.
  - If you like, add one crushed clove of garlic, although the classic kebapche has none.
plating: Three kebapcheta on a wooden board, a spoonful of lyutenitsa, a roasted pepper and a small mound of onion with parsley.
serving: With fresh bread or flatbread and cold beer or ayran.
storage: Raw – up to 24 hours in the refrigerator or up to 3 months in the freezer. Cooked – up to 2 days in the refrigerator.
