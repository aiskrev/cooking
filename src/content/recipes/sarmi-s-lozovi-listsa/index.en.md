mise:
  - Rinse the leaves of their brine and cut off the tough stalks.
  - Chop the onion and the herbs as finely as possible.
  - Wash the rice until the water runs clear and drain it.
technique: Rolling with the sides folded inwards – tight enough that the sarma does not unroll, and loose enough that the rice has room to swell. Simmering is under a weight that keeps the sarmi from floating up.
temperature: The lowest heat, 45–50 minutes under a lid. The mince must be fully cooked, and the rice tender to the core.
balance: The lemon and the yogurt freshen the mince, and the spearmint and the dill give the cool, summery aroma. The leaves are salty – the filling is salted moderately.

steps:
  - title: Prepare the ingredients
    text: Lay out the vine leaves, the mince, the rice, the onion, the herbs, the lemon and the yogurt.
  - title: Blanch the leaves
    text: Dip the leaves in several batches in boiling water for 1–2 minutes, lift them out and leave them to drain.
    why: Blanched leaves soften, do not tear when rolled and lose the excess salt.
  - title: Mix the filling
    text: Knead by hand the mince, the raw rice, the onion, the dill, the spearmint, half of the oil, the paprika, the salt and the black pepper. Add 50 ml of the water.
    cue: The filling is soft, uniform and slightly sticks to the fingers.
  - title: Put the filling on the leaf
    text: Spread a leaf with the veins facing up and the base towards you. Put a full teaspoon of filling near the base.
    avoid: More filling is not better – the sarma bursts when the rice swells.
  - title: Roll
    text: Fold the bottom edge over the filling, then the two sides inwards, and roll up towards the tip of the leaf.
    why: The folded sides close the sarma like an envelope and the filling does not come out while simmering.
    cue: The sarma is firm but gives under light pressure.
  - title: Arrange in the pot
    text: Cover the bottom of a wide pot with torn and spare leaves. Arrange the sarmi snugly side by side, seam down, in one or two layers. Put a few slices of lemon between them, pour in the remaining oil and the hot water.
    why: The leaves on the bottom protect the lower layer from burning.
  - title: Let them simmer
    text: Weigh the sarmi down with an upturned plate, put on the lid and let them simmer for 45–50 minutes on the lowest heat.
    cue: The rice is completely tender and the liquid is almost absorbed.
    avoid: On high heat the sarmi unroll and the leaves tear.
    time: 50
  - title: Stir the sauce
    text: Crush the garlic with the salt and stir it into the yogurt and the dill. Keep the sauce in the refrigerator until serving.
  - title: Serve
    text: Leave the sarmi for 15 minutes under the lid, then arrange them on a plate and serve with the cold sauce and a slice of lemon.
tips:
  - Fresh vine leaves are picked young and blanched a little longer – 2–3 minutes.
  - Roll on a board, not in your hand – the sarmi come out uniform.
  - Sarmi are also excellent cold, with the same sauce.
mistakes:
  - Overly tight sarmi – the rice swells and the leaf splits.
  - High heat – the sarmi unroll and the lower layer burns.
  - Lifting the lid at once – without the rest the liquid is not absorbed and the sarmi fall apart when lifted out.
substitutions:
  - "Lenten version: without mince, with 250 g of rice, more onion and a handful of raisins."
  - Sauerkraut leaves instead of vine leaves – extend the simmering to about an hour and a half.
plating: Five or six sarmi arranged snugly, a spoonful of sauce on the side, a sprig of dill and a slice of lemon.
serving: Warm or cool, with the cold yogurt sauce.
storage: Up to 3 days in the refrigerator, in a closed container. Reheat with a spoonful of water over low heat.
