mise:
  - Use fish that has been deep-frozen (at least 24 hours at –20 °C) or that is sold as suitable for raw consumption.
  - Check the fillet with your fingers and remove the bones with tweezers.
  - Coarsely crush the two kinds of pepper and chop the dill.
technique: "Dry curing under weight: the mixture of salt and sugar draws liquid out of the fish, which turns into a brine. The fillet is turned so that it cures evenly."
temperature: Throughout, the fish stays in the refrigerator at no more than 4 °C. Curing takes 36–48 hours.
balance: Salt and sugar in a ratio of three to two give a soft, slightly sweet gravlax. The dill and the citrus zest freshen the oily fish, and the pink pepper adds colour and a light resinous note.

steps:
  - title: Prepare the ingredients
    text: Lay out the fillet, the salt, the sugar, the dill, the citrus and the pepper. Pat the fish dry with kitchen paper.
    photo: 01-gravlaks-stapka-1.webp
  - title: Mix the cure
    text: Stir the salt and the sugar together with the grated zest of the lemon and the orange and with the crushed pink and black pepper.
    photo: 02-gravlaks-stapka-2.webp
  - title: Cover the fish
    text: Put the fillet skin side down on a large piece of cling film. Spread the cure evenly over the flesh and press it in with the palm of your hand, then cover with the dill.
    cue: The whole surface is covered; at the thinner end the layer is thinner.
    photo: 03-gravlaks-stapka-3.webp
  - title: Press
    text: Wrap the fillet tightly in the film, put it in a deep tray and weigh it down with a board and a weight of about a kilogram. Leave it in the refrigerator for 36–48 hours and turn the parcel every 12 hours.
    why: The weight helps the salt penetrate and makes the flesh dense and easy to slice.
    cue: The fish is firm to the touch and has darkened, and there is liquid in the tray.
    photo: 04-gravlaks-stapka-4.webp
  - title: Clean and slice
    text: Unwrap the fillet, wipe off the cure and the dill, rinse it quickly under cold water and dry it very thoroughly. Slice with a long thin knife at a sharp angle into the thinnest slices possible, leaving the skin on the board.
    photo: 05-gravlaks-stapka-5.webp
  - title: Serve
    text: Arrange the slices on a plate and serve them with blinis, soured cream, capers and lemon.
    photo: 06-gravlaks-stapka-6.webp
tips:
  - After 36 hours the gravlax is softer and more delicate, after 48 – firmer and saltier.
  - Half an hour in the freezer before slicing makes thin slices much easier.
mistakes:
  - Fine cooking salt – it cures too quickly and too strongly.
  - Leaving it in the cure for more than three days – the fish becomes hard and over-salted.
substitutions:
  - Trout instead of salmon – thin fillets are ready in 24 hours.
  - A spoonful of gin or vodka, sprinkled on the fish before curing.
plating: The slices folded into roses, with blinis on the side, a spoonful of soured cream, a few capers and a sprig of dill.
serving: As a cold starter, with blinis or thinly toasted rye bread.
storage: Uncut and well wrapped – up to 4 days in the refrigerator. Raw fish is not recommended for pregnant women, small children and people with weakened immunity.
