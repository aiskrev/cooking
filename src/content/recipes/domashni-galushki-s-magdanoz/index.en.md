steps:
  - title: Make the batter
    text: Beat the eggs with the milk and the salt, add the flour and the nutmeg and stir to a smooth, thick batter – thicker than pancake batter.
    cue: The batter drags slowly off the spoon and does not spread out.
  - title: Rest
    text: Let the batter rest for 15 minutes.
    why: The flour hydrates, and the galushki are softer and do not fall apart.
    time: 15
  - title: Boil the galushki
    text: Bring the water to the boil. Press the batter into small pieces through a large colander, or scrape it off a board with a wet knife straight into the water, in several batches.
    cue: The galushki float to the surface; cook them for another 1–2 minutes.
    time: 5
  - title: Drain and season
    text: Lift them out with a slotted spoon, let them drain and toss them with the butter, the parsley and the pepper.
    avoid: Do not leave them in the water – they overcook and go soft.
tips:
  - Galushki of different sizes are not a problem – that is what makes them truly homemade.
  - Leftovers can be fried in butter until golden.
mistakes:
  - A batter that is too thin – it dissolves in the water.
  - Too many galushki at once – the water stops boiling and they stick together.
substitutions:
  - Dill or parsley butter.
  - Grated kashkaval or sirene on top.
plating: In a warm bowl, with sprigs of parsley on top.
serving: With a stew, kavarma or roast meat.
storage: Up to 2 days in the refrigerator; reheat in a frying pan with a little butter.
