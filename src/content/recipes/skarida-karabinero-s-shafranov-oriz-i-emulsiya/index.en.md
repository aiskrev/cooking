mise:
  - Heat the stock for the rice and keep it over a low flame.
  - Soak the saffron in warm water for 15 minutes.
  - Cube the butter for the emulsion and keep it cold.
  - Get an ice bath ready for the prawns after cleaning.
technique: Separating the heads and shells, a quick sear in butter, flambéing with brandy, extracting flavour from the heads, mounting with cold butter, and a pan rice with saffron.
temperature: The prawns are seared over high heat for 1–2 minutes a side, until the flesh is opaque and reaches 60–63 °C at the core. The emulsion is finished at 70–80 °C without boiling. The rice simmers for 16–18 minutes.
balance: Tomato heightens the sweetness of the prawn, brandy gives depth and saffron colour and aroma. Salt goes in at the end, because the emulsion is concentrated.

steps:
  - title: Lay out the ingredients
    text: Lay out the prawns on ice, the tomatoes, rice, saffron, brandy, stock and butter.
  - title: Prepare the mise en place
    text: "Measure everything into small bowls: saffron, rice, butter, chopped shallot and tomatoes, brandy."
  - title: Clean the prawns
    text: Carefully twist off the heads, keeping them whole. Peel the tails, leaving the last segment on, and remove the intestinal tract. Keep the heads and shells for the emulsion. Hold the tails in the refrigerator.
    why: The heads hold the most flavour – the emulsion is made from them.
  - title: Cook the saffron rice
    text: Sweat the shallot in the olive oil, add the rice and toast it for a minute. Add the hot stock in stages and stir only occasionally. Add the saffron with its water after 10 minutes. After 16–18 minutes, stir in the butter and salt.
    cue: The grains are tender with a slightly firm centre and the rice is creamy.
    time: 18
  - title: Flambé the heads and make the emulsion
    text: Fry the heads and shells in the olive oil for 3–4 minutes, pressing on them. Add the shallot, tomatoes and purée, then the brandy, and carefully set it alight. Once the flame dies down, pour in the stock and saffron and simmer for 15 minutes. Strain, pressing hard, reduce to 120 ml and whisk in the cold butter off the heat.
    why: Pressing the heads draws their flavour and colour into the sauce.
    cue: The emulsion is orange, glossy and coats the spoon.
    avoid: Do not flambé under the extractor hood, and keep a lid close by.
    time: 25
  - title: Sear the prawns
    text: Pat the tails dry, season with salt and sear them in a hot pan with olive oil for 1–2 minutes a side. Add the butter and garlic and baste them a few times.
    cue: The flesh is opaque and springs back when pressed.
    avoid: An overcooked prawn turns rubbery.
    time: 4
  - title: Roast the tomatoes
    text: Halve the tomatoes, dress them with olive oil, season and sear them for a minute in the same pan.
    time: 2
  - title: Plate
    text: Pour 2–3 spoonfuls of emulsion into a shallow bowl. Shape a small mound of saffron rice in the centre, lean the prawn against it and add the tomatoes and basil leaves.
  - title: Finish
    text: Place the prawn with tweezers, adjust the garnishes and serve at once.
tips:
  - Buy carabineros frozen at sea – that is how they keep their flavour best.
  - The emulsion can be made an hour ahead and kept warm in a bain-marie.
  - If you have no brandy, use cognac.
mistakes:
  - Searing for too long – the prawn becomes tough.
  - A boiled emulsion – the butter separates.
  - Throwing away the heads – the main flavour of the dish is lost.
substitutions:
  - Instead of carabineros – large tiger prawns with heads.
  - Instead of bomba – risotto rice.
  - Instead of fish stock – a stock made from the prawn shells.
plating: "A pale shallow bowl: an orange pool of emulsion, a small mound of saffron rice in the centre, the prawn curved around the rice, two or three tomato halves and basil leaves."
serving: "At once, hot. Good non-alcoholic pairings: chilled chamomile tea with orange peel, or sparkling water with citrus."
storage: Does not keep once assembled. The emulsion keeps in the refrigerator for up to 1 day and is reheated gently while whisking.
