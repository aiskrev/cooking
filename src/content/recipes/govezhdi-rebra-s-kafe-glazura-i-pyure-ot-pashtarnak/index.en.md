mise:
  - Take the ribs out of the refrigerator an hour ahead and pat dry.
  - Mix the coffee, sugar, salt and pepper.
  - "Peel the baby onions: cover them with boiling water for a minute and the skins slip off easily."
technique: A dry coffee rub, searing, slow braising in wine, reducing the liquid to a glaze, lacquering, parsnip purée, caramelising onions.
temperature: The ribs are seared over high heat for 2–3 minutes per side. They are braised covered in the oven at 150 °C for 3 hours 30 minutes – 4 hours, until the core reaches 92–95 °C and the meat is tender. Glazing is in the oven at 200 °C for 8–10 minutes, basting every 3 minutes.
balance: The ribs are heavy and rich, so the wine and balsamic vinegar in the glaze bring acidity, the purée sweetness and the coffee a bitterness that deepens the flavour.

steps:
  - title: Lay out the ingredients
    text: Arrange the ribs, coffee, spices, parsnip, onions, wine, stock, butter and herbs.
    photo: 01-govezhdi-stapka-1.webp
  - title: Trim and rub the ribs
    text: Trim off excess surface fat and the tough membrane. Rub the ribs generously with the coffee and spice mix.
    photo: 02-govezhdi-stapka-2.webp
  - title: Sear
    text: Heat the oil in a wide pot and sear the ribs on all sides to a dark crust. Lift them out.
    cue: The crust is dark, almost chocolate-coloured, but not burnt.
    avoid: Coffee burns quickly – do not overheat the fat.
    time: 12
    photo: 03-govezhdi-stapka-3.webp
  - title: Braise
    text: In the same pot fry the vegetables and tomato purée for 5 minutes. Pour in the wine and reduce by half. Return the ribs, add the stock, thyme and bay leaf. Cover and braise in the oven at 150 °C for 3 hours 30 minutes – 4 hours.
    cue: The bone turns freely and a fork slides into the meat with no resistance.
    time: 225
    photo: 04-govezhdi-stapka-4.webp
  - title: Make the purée
    text: Dice the parsnip and simmer it in the milk for 15–20 minutes until tender. Blend with the butter, adding milk until velvety. Season.
    cue: The purée is smooth and falls slowly from the spoon.
    time: 20
    photo: 05-govezhdi-stapka-5.webp
  - title: Prepare the onions
    text: Sear the red onion halves cut-side down in the butter until dark. Add the baby onions, sugar and braising liquid and cook for 10 minutes until tender and glossy.
    cue: The onions are soft with dark caramel edges.
    time: 12
  - title: Make the glaze
    text: Skim the fat from the braising liquid, strain it and reduce with the balsamic vinegar to a thick syrup. Off the heat whisk in the cold butter.
    cue: The glaze is glossy and clings to the spoon.
    time: 20
  - title: Lacquer the ribs
    text: Put the ribs in a roasting tin, brush with glaze and roast at 200 °C for 8–10 minutes, basting with the glaze every 3 minutes.
    cue: The surface is dark and mirror-glossy.
    time: 10
    photo: 06-govezhdi-stapka-8.webp
  - title: Plate
    text: Put a large spoonful of purée on the plate, the rib on top, the roasted onions beside it and a little glaze over. Garnish with thyme.
    photo: 07-govezhdi-stapka-9.webp
tips:
  - The ribs are even better the next day – chill them in the liquid and the fat lifts off the surface easily.
  - Parsnips are sweeter after frost; if they taste bitter, cut out the woody core.
mistakes:
  - Braising too briefly – the meat stays tough; there is no shortcut.
  - Fat left in the liquid – the glaze will not turn glossy.
substitutions:
  - Instead of coffee – unsweetened cocoa powder.
  - Instead of parsnip – celeriac or mashed potato.
  - Instead of red wine – dark beer.
plating: "A dark plate: purée as a half-moon, the rib with its bone pointing up, baby onions and half a roasted onion beside it, glaze over the meat."
serving: With a full-bodied red wine (Mavrud, Cabernet Sauvignon).
storage: The braised ribs keep for 4 days in the refrigerator in their liquid; they freeze for up to 3 months.
