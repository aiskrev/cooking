mise:
  - Wash the peppers, remove the stems and seeds and cut them into strips about 2 cm wide.
  - Grate the tomatoes or chop them finely; cut the onion into half-moons.
technique: The peppers are first sweated over dry heat with a little fat to evaporate part of their moisture, and the tomatoes are added afterwards – this way the yahniya is thick, not watery.
temperature: Medium heat, 25–30 minutes, the first 10 under a lid, then without it.
balance: The peppers are sweet, the tomatoes sharp, the sugar smooths the acidity, and the garlic and the parsley add freshness.

steps:
  - title: Sweat the onion and the peppers
    text: Heat the oil in a wide frying pan, sweat the onion for 4 minutes and add the peppers. Cook for 10 minutes under a lid over medium heat.
    time: 10
  - title: Add the tomatoes
    text: Add the tomatoes, the sugar and the salt. Cook without a lid for 15–20 minutes, stirring from time to time.
    cue: The liquid has almost evaporated and the sauce coats the peppers.
    time: 20
  - title: Season
    text: Take the pan off the heat and stir in the paprika and the garlic. Leave for 5 minutes.
    avoid: Paprika burns over the heat and turns bitter.
    time: 5
  - title: Serve
    text: Sprinkle with parsley and serve with white cheese, fresh bread and a tomato salad.
tips:
  - Fleshy peppers (kapia) give a richer yahniya than thin-walled ones.
  - The next day the yahniya is even tastier, cold or reheated.
mistakes:
  - Covered all the time – the yahniya turns watery.
  - Cutting into very small pieces – the peppers fall apart.
substitutions:
  - Add one or two aubergines for a richer version.
  - One hot pepper for spiciness.
plating: In a frying pan or cast-iron dish set on a wooden board, with a plate of cheese and fresh bread beside it.
serving: With white cheese, bread and a shopska or tomato salad.
storage: Up to 3 days in the refrigerator, in a closed container.
