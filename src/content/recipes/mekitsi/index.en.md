mise:
  - The yogurt and the egg must be at room temperature.
  - Prepare a wide, deep frying pan and a plate lined with kitchen paper.
technique: A soft yeast dough that proves until doubled, is shaped with oiled hands and is fried in enough oil to float.
temperature: "The oil is at 170–175 °C: a piece of dough floats at once and is surrounded by bubbles. Each mekitsa is fried for 1–2 minutes on each side."
balance: The mekitsa itself is neutral and lightly salted – that is why it goes equally well with cheese and with jam.

steps:
  - title: Prepare the ingredients
    text: Lay out the flour, the yogurt, the egg, the yeast, the sugar, the salt and the water.
    photo: 01-mekitsi-stapka-1.webp
  - title: Mix the dough
    text: Dissolve the yeast and the sugar in the warm water. Add the yogurt, the egg and the salt, and then the flour – in parts, stirring with a spoon.
    cue: The dough is soft and still sticks to the bowl.
    photo: 02-mekitsi-stapka-2.webp
  - title: Knead
    text: Transfer the dough to a floured work surface and knead it for 8–10 minutes.
    cue: The dough is smooth, elastic and barely sticky.
    avoid: Do not add a lot of flour – a stiff dough gives tough mekitsi.
    time: 10
    photo: 03-mekitsi-stapka-3.webp
  - title: Let it prove
    text: Put the dough in a greased bowl, cover it with a cloth and leave it in a warm place for 60–90 minutes.
    cue: The volume has doubled and when you press it with a finger the dent springs back slowly.
    time: 75
    photo: 04-mekitsi-stapka-4.webp
  - title: Shape
    text: Oil your hands, pinch off balls the size of a small apple and stretch them into flat rounds – thin in the middle and thicker around the edge.
    why: The thin middle fries quickly and becomes crisp, while the thick edge stays fluffy.
    photo: 05-mekitsi-stapka-5.webp
  - title: Fry
    text: Heat the oil to 170–175 °C. Drop in two or three mekitsi at a time and fry them for 1–2 minutes, until the underside is golden.
    avoid: In cold oil the mekitsi absorb fat; in overheated oil they burn outside and stay raw inside.
    time: 2
    photo: 06-mekitsi-stapka-6.webp
  - title: Turn
    text: Turn the mekitsi with a fork and fry them for another 1–2 minutes on the other side.
    cue: Both sides are evenly golden and the mekitsa is puffed up.
    time: 2
    photo: 07-mekitsi-stapka-7.webp
  - title: Serve
    text: Lift the mekitsi onto kitchen paper to drain and serve them warm.
    photo: 08-mekitsi-stapka-8.webp
tips:
  - Do not overcrowd the pan – the oil cools down and the mekitsi absorb fat.
  - The dough can also prove overnight in the refrigerator; in the morning leave it for 30 minutes at room temperature.
mistakes:
  - A stiff dough – the mekitsi do not puff up.
  - Cold oil – they turn greasy and heavy.
substitutions:
  - "A quick version without yeast: 1 tsp bicarbonate of soda in the yogurt; the dough rests for only 30 minutes."
plating: A pile of warm mekitsi on paper or a board, with small bowls of sirene, jam and honey around them.
serving: With sirene and ayran, or with jam, honey and icing sugar.
storage: Best warm. The next day they are reheated briefly in the oven.
allergens:
  - Gluten
  - Eggs
  - Milk and dairy
