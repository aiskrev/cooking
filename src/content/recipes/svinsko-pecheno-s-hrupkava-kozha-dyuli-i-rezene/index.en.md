mise:
  - The day before, score the skin in narrow strips without reaching the meat, rub it with the salt and leave the meat uncovered in the refrigerator overnight.
  - Peel the quinces, remove the seeds and cut into wedges; keep them in lemon water.
  - Cut the fennel into quarters, keeping the base so it does not fall apart.
technique: Dry-salting and drying the skin, slow roasting on a bed of fruit and vegetables, puffing the crackling at high heat, caramelising quince, braising fennel, a sauce from the roasting juices.
temperature: Roast at 160 °C for about 3 hours to 85–88 °C at the core (the shoulder must become tender). Then 20–25 minutes at 230 °C (or under the grill) until the skin puffs. The quince is braised for 15–20 minutes, then caramelised over high heat.
balance: The fatty meat and crisp skin are balanced by the tart quince and apples, while the fennel brings an aniseed freshness. The cider in the sauce gives a light acidity.

steps:
  - title: Lay out the ingredients
    text: Arrange the meat, quinces, apples, fennel, sage, butter and cider.
  - title: Score and salt the skin
    text: Score the skin at 1 cm intervals with a very sharp knife and rub the salt into the cuts. Leave uncovered in the refrigerator overnight.
    why: Dry skin puffs and crackles; wet skin stays leathery.
  - title: Dry and season
    text: The next day wipe off the excess salt and moisture from the skin. Season the underside of the meat with pepper and tuck the sage leaves along the sides.
  - title: Roast slowly
    text: Put the apples, fennel and onion in a roasting tin, pour in the cider and water and set the meat on top, skin up. Roast at 160 °C for about 3 hours, topping up with water if the tin runs dry.
    cue: The core is 85–88 °C and the meat yields when pressed.
    time: 180
  - title: Puff the crackling
    text: Move the meat to another tin and roast at 230 °C for 20–25 minutes until the skin puffs and crisps. Rest for 20 minutes.
    cue: The skin is puffed and sounds hollow when tapped.
    avoid: Watch the last minutes – crackling burns quickly.
    time: 25
  - title: Caramelise the quince
    text: Braise the quince wedges in the water, honey and half the butter under a lid for 15–20 minutes until tender. Remove the lid, add the remaining butter and caramelise over high heat.
    cue: The quince is golden-orange and glossy.
    time: 20
  - title: Braise the fennel
    text: Sear the fennel quarters in the butter until golden, pour in the stock and braise covered for 15 minutes until tender.
    time: 18
  - title: Make the sauce
    text: Strain the juices from the tin, skim off the fat, add the cider and stock and reduce to a glossy sauce. Whisk in the cold butter off the heat.
    cue: The sauce coats the spoon.
    time: 15
  - title: Carve and plate
    text: Carve the roast into thick pieces with a serrated knife so the crackling does not shatter. Arrange with the quince and fennel, pour the sauce around the meat and garnish with a sage leaf.
tips:
  - Carve skin-side down – the serrated knife goes through more easily.
  - If the skin does not puff evenly, put just the skin under the grill for a few minutes.
mistakes:
  - Covering the meat while it roasts – the skin steams and will not crackle.
  - Sauce on the crackling – it softens within seconds.
substitutions:
  - Instead of quince – pears or firm apples.
  - Instead of shoulder – pork belly or leg with the skin on.
  - Instead of cider – dry white wine.
plating: "A dark plate: a thick piece of roast with the crackling up, two wedges of quince and a quarter of fennel beside it, sauce under the meat."
serving: With mashed or roast potatoes and a glass of cider or white wine.
storage: The roast keeps for 3 days in the refrigerator; reheat in the oven at 200 °C so the skin crisps again.
