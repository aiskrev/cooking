mise:
  - The day before, salt the legs with the coarse salt, thyme and garlic and leave them in the refrigerator for 6–8 hours.
  - Remove the skin from two legs – it will become a crisp crackling.
  - Prepare a small rectangular mould or rings lined with cling film for pressing.
technique: Salting and confiting legs in fat, shredding and pressing the meat, searing the pressed confit, skin crackling, searing breasts, a dark roast-bone jus, chestnut purée and caramelised pears.
temperature: The confit cooks in the fat at 90–95 °C (oven at 120 °C) for 2 hours 30 minutes. The skin for the crackling is baked between two trays at 180 °C for 15–20 minutes. The breasts are finished to 64–65 °C at the core.
balance: The confit is salty and rich, so the pear and cabbage add freshness and the chestnut purée sweetness. The sauce should have a clear acidity from the wine.

    text: Arrange the guinea fowl, chestnuts, pears, cabbage, butter, the fat for the confit and the herbs.

  - title: Cook the confit
    text: Rinse the salt off the legs and pat dry. Cover them with the melted fat and cook in the oven at 120 °C for about 2 hours 30 minutes.
    cue: The meat comes away from the bone at a light touch.
    time: 150

  - title: Press the confit
    text: Pull the meat from the bones and shred it. Mix with the shallot, parsley and 2 tbsp of the fat. Pack tightly into the mould, cover and press under a weight in the refrigerator overnight. Cut into cylinders or rectangles.
    why: Pressing binds the meat so it sears as one piece.
  - title: Make the sauce
    text: Roast the bones until dark, sweat the shallot, deglaze with the wine, add the stock and reduce to a thick, glossy sauce. Strain.
    cue: The sauce coats the spoon.
    time: 45
    photo: 04-gardi-stapka-5.webp

    text: Place the breasts skin-side down in a cold pan with the oil, bring up the heat and sear for 6–7 minutes. Turn and add the butter and thyme. Sear the confit on both sides until crisp as well.

tips:
  - Strain the confit fat and reuse it for potatoes.
  - The confit presses best while the meat is still warm.
  - The skin crackling can be made up to 2 days ahead and kept in a dry container.
mistakes:
  - Salt not rinsed off the legs – the confit becomes too salty.
  - Too high a heat when searing the confit – it falls apart before it crusts.
substitutions:
  - Instead of duck fat – lard or olive oil (lighter flavour).
  - Instead of guinea fowl – free-range chicken or duck (cook the breasts to 56–58 °C).

serving: Straight away, with a full-bodied white or a light red wine.
storage: The pressed confit keeps for up to 5 days in the refrigerator; the sauce for up to 3 days.
